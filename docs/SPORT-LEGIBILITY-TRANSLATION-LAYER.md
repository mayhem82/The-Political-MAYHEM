# Sport Legibility Translation Layer

Status: **ACTIVE — CANONICAL PUBLIC TRANSLATION LAYER**

## Purpose

The Political MAYHEM uses sport as the public translation layer for political legibility.

The objective is not to trivialise politics or claim that politics is literally sport. The objective is to expose the continuously changing political environment in a grammar that is already widely understood: teams, players, form, selection, rivalries, fixtures, momentum, tipping, live state, outcomes and post-event review.

The underlying evidence architecture remains political-domain native, provenance-controlled and auditable. Sport is the interface grammar; evidence control remains the truth layer.

**Sport for legibility. Evidence architecture for truth control.**

## Missing-layer hypothesis

Conventional political coverage is fragmented across speeches, polling, policy announcements, parliamentary events, personalities, scandals, campaigns, institutional decisions and elections. The reader is usually required to reconstruct the competitive state of the political environment themselves.

Sport normally provides this state directly.

A sports audience can rapidly understand:

- who the teams are;
- who the players are;
- current form;
- selection and availability;
- leadership changes;
- rivalries and alliances;
- the next contest;
- live movement;
- current tipping position;
- verified result;
- historical performance;
- post-event audit.

The Political MAYHEM applies this legibility structure to politics while preserving the political evidence beneath it.

## Canonical translation

- **Team → Party**
- **Player → Political actor**
- **Captain / leadership group → Party leader and senior leadership actors**
- **Team form → Party political form**
- **Player form → Actor political form**
- **Selection change → Candidate, ministerial, shadow-ministerial or party-role change**
- **Injury / availability → Actor availability, eligibility, suspension, withdrawal, resignation or incapacity state**
- **Fixture / match → Defined political tipping target**
- **Season → Political cycle**
- **Ladder / standings → Current structural political position, where a defensible metric exists**
- **Rivalry → Persistent competitive political relationship**
- **Tactical alliance → Coalition, preference, confidence-and-supply or issue-specific alignment**
- **Home-ground / venue context → Electorate, jurisdiction, chamber or institutional context**
- **Score / result → Verified political outcome**
- **Tip → Public MAYHEM selected outcome**
- **Odds / confidence → Probability or confidence attached to a tip**
- **Pre-match watch → Forward evidence accumulation before a target closes**
- **Kickoff / close → Target-specific evidence freeze point**
- **Post-match review → Outcome verification, grading and failure audit**
- **Season record → Longitudinal party/actor/contest performance history**

## Continuous-field rule

Politics does not become live only when an election is called.

The political field is always active. Party and actor intelligence accumulate continuously. Discrete outcome-bearing targets emerge from that live field and receive their own tipping clocks, freezes, outcomes and audits.

Therefore:

**continuous political field → team/player state → emerging target → MAYHEM tip → freeze → verified outcome → audit → learned state → continuous field**

Multiple tipping targets may coexist.

## Public-legibility model

The public surface should answer, at a glance:

1. Which teams are currently strongest, weakest, rising or falling?
2. Which players are driving the movement?
3. What changed since the previous checkpoint?
4. Which relationships, alliances, conflicts or institutional events are materially affecting the field?
5. What outcome-bearing contests are currently open?
6. What is the current MAYHEM tip for each target?
7. What evidence moved that tip?
8. When does that tip freeze?
9. What was the verified result?
10. Was the tip correct, incorrect, partial or void, and why?

This is the principal public translation function of The Political MAYHEM.

## Boundary conditions

The sport grammar must never overwrite political reality.

- Political parties remain political parties in canonical data.
- Political actors remain political actors in canonical data.
- Coalitions are relationships unless the underlying political structure genuinely creates a single electoral entity.
- Independents are not forced into fictional teams.
- Polling is not automatically equivalent to a scoreboard; metric meaning must be explicit.
- A political event becomes a tipping target only when a defined outcome can later be verified.
- No sports metaphor may lower evidence standards, erase uncertainty or simplify a political mechanism beyond what the evidence supports.

## Architecture boundary

The translation layer sits above the evidence and intelligence architecture.

**Canonical political data → provenance/evidence → intelligence state → analytical/tipping engine → sport-legibility translation → public interface**

The translation layer may rename or organise concepts for comprehension, but it must not change canonical evidence, timestamps, lineage, grades or uncertainty.

## Design consequence

The Political MAYHEM public site should increasingly resemble a live sports intelligence product in navigational logic and information density:

- Live Field
- Teams
- Players
- Form
- Tipping
- Current Targets
- Signals
- Standings / structural position where valid
- Audit Clock
- Results
- Historical record

The public experience should make the political environment legible without requiring the user to reconstruct its state from disconnected political reporting.
